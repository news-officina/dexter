Russia vetoes US draft as UN mission in Western Sahara expires on 31 October

The Security Council failed to renew MINURSO's mandate for the first time, after Moscow blocked an American text that would have extended the mission to 2027 and removed its referendum role.

31 October 2026

For the first time, the Security Council failed to renew MINURSO's mandate in Western Sahara, and the mission expires on 31 October. The US draft drew 11 votes, but Russia vetoed it. Russia's own draft failed with 3 votes and 11 abstentions, short of the 9 required, so the American negative vote did not count as a veto.

The two drafts set a rewritten mandate against a technical extension. The US draft would have extended the mandate to 31 October 2027 and removed MINURSO's role in organizing a referendum. It asked the Secretary-General for proposals on the mission's structure by 30 January 2027. Moscow called on reasonable members to support it.

Vassily Nebenzia called his veto a 'veto of salvation' and his draft the last chance to keep MINURSO intact, warning opponents would bear responsibility. The US delegate answered that the veto preserved the letter R for a referendum the UN has not prepared for decades. She said the American text would have supported self-determination and moved the 50-year conflict forward.

In a 22 October letter, Polisario's UN representative Sidi Omar said the US zero draft could not serve as a basis for deliberations on MINURSO or the peace process. He threatened to withdraw from the process altogether if it were adopted without substantive changes. He wrote that the draft fully reflected Morocco's position. On 31 October, Algeria expressed 'profound regret'. It blamed the outcome on the US draft's 'excessive bias' in favour of Morocco.

France's delegate warned that an abrupt closure would be exceedingly alarming in the context of resumed tensions. The United States said it stands ready to work with the Secretary-General on an orderly drawdown that protects UN personnel and addresses security and humanitarian needs. In a Friday evening statement, the Royal Cabinet said the mission's presence in the southern provinces should cease in the coming days. It said non-renewal should end the Council's seizure of the file.
