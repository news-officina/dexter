CMPorts overtakes COSCO as Europe counts Chinese stakes in its ports

A study for the European Parliament put Chinese control at about 10% of European container throughput, and the two leading firms answer to the same state.

31 October 2026

CMPorts has overtaken COSCO Shipping Ports as the number two in global terminal-operator rankings, according to Lloyd's List, which reported the shift in December 2025 and described both as state-owned operators. Both answer to the same state. A Hong Kong Exchange filing records that China Merchants Port Investment Development holds a 45.96% interest in China Merchants Port Group, the listed issuer's controlling shareholder.

Chinese state equity already spans terminals from Marseille where COSCO Shipping Ports holds 8% of Eurofos, to Piraeus where it owns all of Piers 2 and 3. In between sit Hamburg Tollerort at 25%, Rotterdam Euromax at 35% and Valencia CSP at 51%. A study for the European Parliament put Chinese control at about 10% of European container throughput.

In July 2016 COSCO bought a 51% majority stake in the Piraeus Port Authority and became its main decision maker. The concession runs to 2052, with a €350 million investment commitment over ten years and the land not transferred to COSCO. The consequences showed in the traffic. A difference-in-differences study published in June 2023, working from 2015 to 2019 vessel tracking data, found that container ship calls at Piraeus had increased after privatization. The increase was led by COSCO-operated vessels above 3000 TEU. Medium and large vessels drove a roughly 5 percentage point increase in the probability that a COSCO ship would call.

An EU study counted 24 Chinese acquisition deals in European maritime infrastructure between 2004 and 2021, with more than €9.1 billion invested. Athens drew its own line years later. Greek authorities blocked COSCO from rolling out the Hellenic Port Community System, saying Greek freight forwarders' information would be handed to COSCO.

Enough operators, no control

ISL has set out the test it thinks matters. Where a port offers a sufficient choice of terminal operators, it argues, no single actor gains strategic control. Its own count puts COSCO Shipping Ports at equity stakes in 7 terminals among the EU's top 30 ports, which handle 87% of EU container traffic. Clingendael, for its part, notes that in France, China Merchants Port Holdings holds only minority stakes in container terminals.

HHLA rejects the warning. Key decisions remain with HHLA, the terminal open to all customers, COSCO holds no strategic know-how, German law applies, and the deal creates no one-sided dependencies. In mid-2023, Chinese state-owned players held majority control in only two European ports, Piraeus and Zeebrugge, according to an industry report from February 2025. COSCO Shipping Ports owned 90% of CSP Zeebrugge, 100% of Piraeus Terminals 1 and 2, and 67% of the Piraeus Port Authority.

The position of the German economics ministry is on the record. In October 2022 it permitted COSCO only a share below 25% in the Tollerort terminal, ruling that anything higher would trigger a new investment screening. The ministry barred COSCO from veto rights over strategic business or personnel decisions, from appointing management, and from gaining further control. The final approval, at 24.9%, came in 2023 after an investment screening begun in 2021, contrary to the advice of competent institutions, according to reports from HHLA, Insurance Day and ISL.

Screening regulation 2019/452

The European Parliament and the Council jointly enacted Regulation 2019/452 on 19 March 2019, establishing a framework for screening foreign direct investments into the Union. The state question is not in dispute: index providers added COSCO SHIPPING Holdings to state-owned-enterprise dividend indexes in December 2024. The indexes include the SSE State-owned Enterprises Dividend Index and the CSI Central State-owned Enterprises Dividend 50 Index, which the company says indicates its status as a central state-owned enterprise.

COSCO SHIPPING Holdings names geopolitical and policy changes as a risk to its globalisation goals and maintains a country risk assessment mechanism. In the Piraeus HPCS case, researchers judged fears of Chinese control of port information spaces debatable, and PCT said communication was visible only to the two parties.
