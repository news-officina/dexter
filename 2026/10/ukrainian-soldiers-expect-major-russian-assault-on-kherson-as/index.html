Ukrainian soldiers expect major Russian assault on Kherson as ceasefire ends on 1 Nov

Soldiers report KamAZ lorries moving combat units non-stop and Grad launchers massing near Radensk during the 80-hour truce, with the deputy governor warning of far heavier shelling from Monday. A civilian evacuation of about 2,000 residents in Oleshky begins Saturday.

31 October 2026

Ukrainian soldiers expect Russia to launch a major assault on Kherson the moment the ceasefire ends on 1 November. The forest near Radensk has been packed with Grad rocket launchers, KamAZ lorries are moving combat units non-stop, and the soldiers say Russia may cross the Dnipro once the truce ends.

Melania Trump declared on 31 October that 80 hours of ceasefire were underway, with aid being delivered and a civilian evacuation convoy prepared in Oleshky. The Telegraph reported on 30 October that Russia appears to be using that same pause to prepare its Kherson assault.

The evidence is not abstract. Ukrainian soldiers report that trees near Radensk are being felled, timber taken away and dugouts reinforced, according to Bild, Shelter and The Telegraph. They describe Russian KamAZ lorries carrying combat units moving non-stop, which they read as preparation for a major attack on Kherson. Ukrainian personnel in the Kherson sector are using drone cameras to track the movement of Russian units, and the footage shows extensive preparations.

Bild and Shelter report Russian drone units being moved closer to the Dnipro, which one Ukrainian soldier said may indicate crossing attempts once the ceasefire ends. Kherson's deputy governor said: "We are very concerned that from Monday the Russians will start shelling Kherson heavily again." He continued: "This ceasefire gave them time to bring in military equipment, resupply their forces and rotate their units. So we expect that from Monday the shelling of Kherson could become much more intensive."

The main phase of the humanitarian mission in Oleshky started on 30 October, with ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets saying the convoy delivered aid that morning after routes were demined. About 2,000 residents remain in the town, to be evacuated from Saturday, according to Bild and The Independent. Not all of them know an evacuation is coming, because internet access there is gone.
