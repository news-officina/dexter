Nordic defence firms call for joint missile warning and Arctic intelligence

Six companies behind the Nordic Compass Defence Statement ask the five governments to join a shared space-based missile warning system. Industry says budgets alone will not deliver capability without an integrated Nordic defence market.

6 November 2026

Six Nordic defence companies published the Nordic Compass Defence Statement in Gothenburg on 5 November. The Nordic Compass Defence Statement asks for a space-based joint missile warning system, Arctic intelligence and secure communications linking national and tactical networks. It also sets four first steps, from a joint capability roadmap to a pilot contract that pays for production capacity.

Panu Routila, Patria's President and CEO, said the Nordic countries spend at record levels and co-operate more than ever. Yet they still run five separate, somewhat differently implemented procurement chains, and he said closer Nordic cooperation must help deliver capabilities faster. All five are now NATO Allies and are significantly increasing investment in defence and resilience, the companies state. KONGSBERG argued that budgets alone are not enough, and that an integrated Nordic defence market makes the region safer and drives the economy.

Ericsson, Kongsberg, Nokia, Patria and Saab launched the Nordic Compass Defence Track in August 2026 to strengthen security in Europe. The statement invites the governments of Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden, in particular their defence authorities and defence forces, to join in developing the initiatives.

Nordic Compass, the Nordic Round Table for industry led by Finland's former Prime Minister Jyrki Katainen, was launched on 12 May 2026. It gathers 30 leading Nordic companies, foundations and organisations and covers four tracks: Capital Markets, Deep Tech, Defence and Energy. The Defence Track within it is chaired by Jyri Häkämies, former Defence Minister of Finland.

The question that remains is commitment. Joachim Finkielman said in March that political will and cross-border industry cooperation exist, but momentum will not materialise until leaders commit to joint programmes. Those three presented the alliance's first initiatives at the Nordic Compass Summit in Gothenburg on 4-5 November.
