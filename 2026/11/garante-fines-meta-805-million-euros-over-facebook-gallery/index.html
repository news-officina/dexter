Garante fines Meta 805 million euros over Facebook gallery access

The Garante ordered specific consent for the feature, which was on by default for roughly 20 million Android users in Italy. Meta says it will appeal.

14 November 2026

Italy's data-protection authority, the Garante, has fined Meta Platforms Ireland 805 million euros, about 0.47 percent of Meta's group global revenue, over a Facebook feature that suggested photos and videos from Android galleries. Roughly 20 million Android users in Italy were affected. Meta must seek separate, specific consent for the feature, keep it off by default and report back within 90 days. Meta says it will appeal.

Meta had argued the case belongs with the Irish authority. The Authority rejected that objection because ePrivacy has no one-stop-shop mechanism. Those rules stay where they are for now: on 21 October member states agreed a negotiating position on the Digital Omnibus that keeps the cookie and device-access consent rules inside the ePrivacy Directive, with six exemptions and a 24-month transposition period.

The sanctioned measure is the Facebook feature 'Get tips from your gallery while browsing Facebook', available since 2023. According to the Garante, Meta accessed through the Android app not only these files but also the metadata attached to them. It did so without specific consent and without informing users properly. The Authority's checks found gallery access rests on a single permission valid for the entire app and multiple functions. The generic notice 'enable other features' did not tell users they were also authorizing gallery suggestions, and the feature was active by default.

In setting the fine, the Authority cited the violation's duration, still ongoing for users who have not updated the app. It also cited a previous 2019 ruling against Meta and what it called a lack of cooperation during the investigation.

The company said it strongly disagreed with the Italian authority's assessment, its application of the law, and the fine's level, which it called disproportionate. It described the feature as simple, noting users must grant permission on their device before it operates and can change that permission in their settings.
