Russia strikes Zaporizhzhia aid point after Kyiv virtually ran out of interceptors

Zelensky says the death toll could approach 100 after the double-tap attack on Komyshuvakha, the deadliest strike on the region since the full-scale invasion. Latvia has called an extraordinary UN Security Council meeting.

6 November 2026

President Volodymyr Zelensky said on 5 November that 43 bodies had been recovered in Komyshuvakha and 53 people were still missing. He said the death toll could approach 100. Russian air strikes on 2 November hit Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region, striking a former school used as an aid distribution point and a nearby shelter where civilians had gathered. First they hit the crowds waiting for aid, then rescuers and police officers who had come to help.

The strike came as Kyiv had virtually run out of Western-supplied interceptors. Moscow stepped up attacks after trilateral talks were suspended in February 2026, the BBC reported. Finland's Defense Minister Antti Hakkanen said European countries should commit at least 0.25% of GDP to Ukraine. Kyiv has identified that level as the minimum to stay financially afloat. US envoy Steve Witkoff said on Monday that Washington will bring the Ukrainian and Russian delegations to Abu Dhabi on 10-11 November to discuss ending the conflict, postwar security and de-escalation.

The strike did not catch anyone unaware. Danielle Bell, head of the U.N. Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine, said the timing and location of the aid distribution had been well publicized. The attack, she said, "seems to have violated the most fundamental principles of international humanitarian law." Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights Michael O'Flaherty strongly condemned the double-tap strike. "When deliberately directed against civilian targets in violation of international humanitarian law, such strikes amount to war crimes," O'Flaherty said.

Mykhailo Semikin, head of the Zaporizhzhia regional military administration, said the Komyshuvakha attack was the deadliest Russian strike on the region since the full-scale invasion. Search and rescue work went on for days, with rescuers and canine units from several regions brought in under armored vehicles, the State Emergency Service reported. Its work was repeatedly suspended: Russian drones struck the bombed area again and hindered the effort. Ukrainian authorities said those follow-up attacks injured at least 17 people.

Latvia, holding the UN Security Council presidency for November 2026, has called an extraordinary meeting of the council over the strike, Foreign Minister Baiba Braže announced on 3 November. "With these killings, Russia is sending an entirely opposite signal: Russia is counting on more killings," he said.
