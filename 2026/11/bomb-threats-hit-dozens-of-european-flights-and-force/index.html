Bomb threats hit dozens of European flights and force Irish newsrooms to evacuate

Italy's anti-terrorism prosecutors now coordinate investigations in four cities, as Spain's foreign minister calls the campaign an attempt at hybrid aggression and Dublin judges it probably coordinated.

6 November 2026

A fresh wave of bomb alerts disrupted air traffic across Europe on Friday 6 November and forced newsrooms in Ireland and elsewhere to evacuate their staff. Hungary scrambled Gripen jets and Bulgaria dispatched MiGs to escort planes that had been targeted, and the affected aircraft all landed safely. The Irish evacuations followed threatening emails sent to RTÉ, the Irish Independent, Newstalk and The Irish Mirror.

Italy's National Anti-Mafia and Anti-Terrorism Prosecutor's Office, led by Raniero Altavilla, took charge on 6 November of coordinating investigations in Rome, Milan, Busto Arsizio and Civitavecchia. In Dublin, the government of Taoiseach Micheál Martin judged it probable that the alerts were a coordinated campaign meant to cause disruption.

The campaign began on Monday 2 November, when a Wizz Air flight from London to Sofia was diverted to Budapest. In the days that followed, dozens of European flights, Aer Lingus and Ryanair among them, along with some supermarkets and Irish media offices, received bomb threats. Spanish Foreign Minister José Manuel Albares said the most reasonable assumption was that Europe faced an attempt at hybrid aggression and psychological warfare. Europe, he said, must be prepared for more such challenges.

Threats against two flights landing in Rome came from an anti-feminist group calling itself The Male State. It claimed it had planted explosives on a Finnair flight from Helsinki and an Aer Lingus flight from Dublin. The BBC reported that the hoax email was written in word-perfect Italian and sent on 5 November at 07:21 to Aeroporti di Roma. Its originating address seemed linked to Bogotá's state health services. Vladislav Pozdniakov, who founded the Russian far-right misogynist movement, denied on Telegram that the group was involved, saying the movement does not exist since 2021.

Investigators said there was no evidence that the hoax threats were orchestrated by a hostile terror group or a state actor such as Russia or Iran. Inquiries were continuing. European and NATO officials had warned that Russia was escalating its campaign of so-called hybrid warfare against Europe.
