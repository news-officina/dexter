EU blacklists 1,569 Russian military suppliers in what Council calls largest round

The listings bring the total under the 2014 regime to nearly 4,600, with work on a further sanctions package continuing. Brussels says it will not follow Washington's easing of sanctions on Russian diesel.

3 November 2026

On 12 October 2026 the Council adopted restrictive measures against 1,569 individuals and entities in Russia's military and industrial complex, a round it described as the EU's largest-ever. The listings bring the total under the regime to close to 4,600.

The Council calls the listed mid-level suppliers the core of Russia's military production, with turnover of €20 billion and 250,000 employees. They supply key components for Russia's missiles, drones and aircraft, including semiconductors, explosives, ammunition and navigation systems. Each now faces an asset freeze, a ban on providing funds or economic resources, and a travel ban to the EU for their natural persons.

The legal basis is Council decision (CFSP) 2026/2279 and Council implementing regulation (EU) 2026/2281, both of 12 October 2026, which amend the 2014 sanctions regime concerning Ukraine's territorial integrity. The two acts introduce no new sectoral, trade, energy or financial restrictions. Work on a further 22nd sanctions package continues. EU ambassadors agreed the listings on 7 October 2026, before ministers formally adopted them on 12 October, after several weeks of deliberation and legal review.

Washington has eased one measure on Russian diesel. Brussels has not. On 9 October, after a reported US-Russia agreement on diesel supplies, the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) issued General License 135 authorising certain transactions involving Russian-origin diesel until 7 April 2027. Other US sanctions remain in place. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen told EU lawmakers the EU will not ease sanctions on Russian fossil fuels despite that deal. Russia, she said, "is not a trusted supplier of energy because it would, this is our experience, at any time weaponize energy dependencies to blackmail others."

The European Parliament has scheduled a plenary debate on procedure 2026/2949(RSP), 'Further EU sanctions against Russia in response to the escalating attacks against Ukraine: cutting of the sources financing the Russian war', for 12 November 2026 as agenda point D-51. The 12 October listings respond in particular to Russia's recent military campaign deliberately targeting civilian infrastructure and causing severe suffering to the civilian population.
