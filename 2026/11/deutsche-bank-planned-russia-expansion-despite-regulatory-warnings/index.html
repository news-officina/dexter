Deutsche Bank planned Russia expansion before the invasion despite warnings, leaks show

Leaked documents show the bank courted oligarchs and its Russian subsidiary made a €61 million pre-tax profit last year. German Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil has called for full transparency.

18 November 2026

Leaked documents show Deutsche Bank put commercial interests ahead of sanctions and anti-money-laundering rules, courted oligarchs and planned to expand in Russia before the 2022 invasion. Deutsche Bank says it is now "a different bank", citing €7 billion invested in controls since 2019.

German Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil called for full transparency on its control failings and how it has remedied them. He said it falls to the independent regulators and law enforcement authorities to examine what emerged, and stated his certainty they would do so.

Deutsche Bank still employed 162 people in Russia last year, and its subsidiary there made a pre-tax profit of €61 million. Before the invasion, the Tamedia newspapers report, Deutsche Bank held the fortunes of some twenty oligarchs close to Vladimir Putin in Zurich and Geneva.

Documents show that at the end of 2020 Deutsche Bank planned to sharply increase revenue from wealthy Russian clients despite internal and external warnings about regulatory and money-laundering risks. Chief executive Christian Sewing was among the members of the working group that drew up the strategy. The same documents show contested accounting and tension inside the compliance teams, at times over the bank's own procedures. Deutsche Bank shares fell almost six percent in Frankfurt after the reports appeared.

Deutsche Bank answered that the allegations are not new. The bank said it had, as a precaution, informed the relevant authorities, including the data protection authority, and remains in contact with them. It pointed to ten credit rating upgrades by leading rating agencies over the past six years. Deutsche Bank says the upgrades show a stronger, safer bank.
