Parliament debates cyber resilience as member states fight for control of CSA 2

The Irish Presidency has tabled a compromise on who decides cyber security powers, with the debate set for Wednesday at 15:00. Parliament has already demanded sovereign cyber detection and response capabilities by wide majorities.

3 November 2026

The European Parliament debates cyber resilience of critical infrastructure in plenary on Wednesday at 15:00, and the fight underneath it is about who decides. Member states are pushing for decision-making power over the CSA 2 to sit with themselves rather than in Brussels.

By November 2026 the Irish Council Presidency had tabled a new compromise proposal for the second Cybersecurity Act, and the negotiation now sits mid-stream.

Parliament set out its demand in the September report on hybrid threats, which calls for Europe to build sovereign cyber capabilities for detection, attribution and response. It passed 470 for, 129 against, 62 abstentions. The backdrop is live: Ukrainian attacks struck data centers of Yandex, the Russian technology giant, in October 2026.

On 15 September the chamber adopted the Democracy Shield report by 420 votes to 220, with 26 abstentions. The report called for defence of democratic institutions and a stronger EU toolbox. The next day the chamber adopted a report warning that hybrid attacks against the EU are increasing, with Russia and its proxies the main culprits.

The investigation question now sits with the Commission. Sandro Gozi, a member of the European Parliament, said the Commission must urgently investigate whether the disruption had a measurable impact on internet political activity in Germany, Italy, France and other member states. Cloudflare Radar data showed automated traffic from Yandex networks fell to 0.4% of its maximum of the previous two weeks, and whether that drop weakened Russian influence operations against European democracies is what the Commission must now establish.
