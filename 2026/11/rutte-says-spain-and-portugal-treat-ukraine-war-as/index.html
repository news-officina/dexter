Rutte says Spain and Portugal treat Ukraine war as a distant Polish concern

The NATO Secretary General said a Russian missile takes five minutes longer to reach Lisbon than Warsaw. Portugal's foreign ministry, led by Paulo Rangel, called the remark light treatment of a serious matter.

11 November 2026

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said in a Dutch television interview that Spain and Portugal treat the war in Ukraine as far away and mainly a Polish concern. He added that Russia's newest missiles take five minutes longer to reach Lisbon than Warsaw. Portugal's foreign ministry, led by Paulo Rangel, answered on X on Monday. It said it was regrettable that Rutte should speak so lightly on such a serious matter.

The remark embraced a thesis defended for months by the Netherlands, Germany, the Nordic countries and the Baltics. Spain, Portugal, France and Italy, it holds, give Kyiv less financial and military help than they should because they see the war as distant. NATO spokesperson Allison Hart answered that all Allies agree Russia is a threat. She said Rutte often explains to public audiences, who may be less engaged on security issues, how close that threat is to every Ally.

The remark came in an interview with a Dutch television programme in which Rutte accused Spain and Portugal of downplaying the Russian threat. He said he had heard the argument from allies in both countries. In the same interview, he said he sometimes answers such allies with the five minutes longer a Russian missile takes to reach Lisbon than Warsaw.

Rutte said in the interview: "This affects us all. We cannot be naive about it. We have to protect ourselves". The warning implied the governments of Pedro Sánchez and Luís Montenegro are not doing enough to support Kyiv or defend Europe from Putin's aggression. The ministry wrote on X that Rutte must know which countries and governments stand firmly on Ukraine's side and know the Russian threat well. It added that Portugal, its government and its people have never been in any doubt about which side of history they stand on.

A Eurobarometer poll taken this spring found that 92 percent of those surveyed in Portugal see Russia's invasion of Ukraine as a security threat to the EU. The EU average was 76 percent, according to Politico. The foreign ministry has reaffirmed its support for the defence of Ukraine.
