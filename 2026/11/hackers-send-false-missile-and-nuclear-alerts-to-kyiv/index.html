Hackers send false missile and nuclear alerts to Kyiv and all Russia in one night

Ukraine's Air Alert app and Russia's Emergency Ministry app were breached hours apart, and both governments restored their systems and denied any real threat. A previously unknown group, Hitcore, claimed the Ukrainian breach.

2 November 2026

Hackers struck the warning systems of two countries in one night. On 1 November they broke into Ukraine's Air Alert app, pushing false warnings of radiation, chemical attack, street fighting in Kyiv and a strike by the Oreshnik, a Russian missile. Hours later users across all Russian regions received false alerts about launched nuclear warheads through the app of Russia's Emergency Situations Ministry.

In Ukraine, the first false notification appeared at 22:47 Kyiv time on 1 November. A minute later, a Russian-language one said a nuclear alert was coming across the whole country and that an Oreshnik missile was on its way. The two false warnings reached wartime publics hours apart in the middle of the night.

Ukraine's institutions answered within hours. The Air Force said the app had likely been compromised by the enemy and refuted any radiation or chemical threat. The developers disabled push notifications, suspended the app, and restored its main functions within two hours, adding security measures as they resumed operation. The app's press service and the Ministry of Digital Transformation said it stores no personal data and reports of hacked accounts were false.

Russia's Emergency Ministry said the app had suffered a massive hacker attack. Its specialists took the affected services offline, isolated them and removed the false information, including from the ministry's regional channels on the Max messenger. The ministry asked the public to remain calm and to trust only official sources. It added that the national alert system works normally, notifications continue through sirens, loudspeakers, text messages, television and radio, the app is only supplementary, and the hack did not and cannot affect the alert system.

Hitcore, a hacking group described as newly founded and previously unknown, said it had breached Ukraine's Air Alert app. The Ukrainian Ministry of Digital Transformation said the app was temporarily disabled, that sirens and general notification systems worked normally, and urged people to enable system emergency notifications on their smartphones.
