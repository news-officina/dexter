New York Times calls Brave a burglar and an AI fence in copyright lawsuit

The complaint says the browser's paywall blocker and News index distribute Times journalism without permission, and that Brave sells unlicensed material through its Search API to AI companies. Brave said separately that Anthropic used its Search API to train Claude 2.

3 November 2026

The New York Times calls Brave a burglar and an AI "fence" in its copyright complaint against the company.

In its complaint, the Times says Brave "acts both like a burglar that misappropriates the content of publishers, and an AI 'fence' that sells stolen content to third parties". In September 2025, the Times alleged the Brave browser shipped with a default "Paywall blocker" that provides paywalled content, including Times journalism, to users.

In September 2025, the Times alleged Brave promoted a specialized "News" index of articles sourced from trusted outlets worldwide, including The Times, using its trademark without authorization. The filing says the index covered hundreds of top publishers. The case also rests on Brave's own statement, from November 2023, that Anthropic, a maker of AI models, had used its Search API to train Claude 2 on high-quality data.

Times spokesperson Graham James said Brave flouted established copyright law and ignored repeated requests to stop its conduct. He said the company believes in the ethical and responsible use and development of AI. In September 2025, three American newspaper publishers, the Chicago Tribune Company, Daily News, LP and Orlando Sentinel Communications Company, filed their own copyright complaint against Brave, demanding a jury trial.

In September 2025, the Times alleged that through the Brave Search API, Brave sells unlicensed copyrighted material, including Times journalism, to other AI companies. The companies use it to train models or answer queries. Perplexity, a generative AI company, answered the suit with its own charge, saying the Times is overpaying its attorneys and underpaying its journalists. Brave, Anthropic and xAI, another maker of AI models, had not responded to requests for comment.
