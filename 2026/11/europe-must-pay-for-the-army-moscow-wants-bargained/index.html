Europe must pay for the army Moscow wants bargained away

While Bulgaria and Slovakia refuse the package, the size of Europe's second-largest army on the continent is being settled in Abu Dhabi without us. Fund it now, every state contributing, or lose the seat at the table.

13 November 2026

In Abu Dhabi, Ukrainian negotiators are asking for ten years of Western support for an army of 800,000; in Brussels, Bulgaria and Slovakia have already refused to join a new package that would close a 70-billion-euro gap for the coming year. We say plainly what that arithmetic means: the size of Europe's second-largest army on the continent is being decided in a Gulf emirate, in talks from which Moscow wants no Europeans at the table. That is not burden-sharing. It is abdication, and it is recent.

The defection is the story. Bulgaria and Slovakia have announced they will not join a new aid package, and Hungary and the Czech Republic are counted as wavering, this a year after the union approved 90 billion euros for 2026 and 2027. Of the 70-billion-euro gap we already know, the Commission puts 45 billion on the EU. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has answered as the moment demands: in a letter to European Council President António Costa he called on all member states without exception to make a fair contribution to closing the gap, and tied the rest of the Council's financial business to a joint commitment by making his assent to other matters depend on one. He wants the multi-year financing settled inside the negotiations on the 2028 to 2034 budget. That is the right demand, weakened only by being made as a request to governments that have learned a summit statement and an exemption are worth the same.

The money is a seat at the table, and ours is empty. Rustem Umerov led Kyiv's delegation; the Russian side came to discuss more than the war. Its delegation calls settling the war one item on an agenda whose main purpose is reviewing the security of the entire continent. Our own officials say such questions can only start after a ceasefire. So the security architecture is being drawn in a room Europe enters last. Ukrainian negotiators have named this before: they warn of a Yalta 2.0, leaders dividing Europe over Europe's head. Ukraine has already rejected a scheme that would let Russia declare the capture of Donbas territory it does not hold. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky says Moscow claims to hold half the Donbas, land that is in fact Ukrainian, and the Americans confirmed it. Only an army we guarantee lets Kyiv keep rejecting it for the ten years asked.

Those who would wait say binding commitments for a war that is being talked to an end are money poured into a truce. Prime that waiting against paying, and it loses: deterrence built again after an army dissolves is paid in years and lives, not euros. The cheapest security Europe will ever buy is the one already standing, and it asks only to be paid.

American money is arriving late and under pressure: Congress had to write to its own administration to demand action on 400 million dollars, about 357 million euros, held up in execution, a sliver of the gap we already know. A partner, not a protector. Bulgaria and Slovakia have already refused the new package, with Hungary and the Czech Republic counted as wavering, and every aid vote inside the budget talks gives them leverage elsewhere. The informal EU summit meets in Dublin on Friday. It should adopt the multi-year financing there, every state contributing, the coming year covered first, so the army we already sized is paid for by Europe and sized by no one else.
