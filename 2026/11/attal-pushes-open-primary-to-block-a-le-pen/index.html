Attal pushes open primary to block a Le Pen and Melanchon run-off in 2027

Marcangeli and Retailleau reject the plan as incoherent while Lisnard backs it. An Ipsos poll has Attal rising and Le Pen slipping, but her 33.5% still leads the field.

1 November 2026

Gabriel Attal proposed a 'grande primaire' to prevent a 2027 second round between Marine Le Pen and Jean-Luc Mélenchon, saying it should 'empêcher un second tour'. Laurent Marcangeli called the plan 'aussi cafouilleuse que hasardeuse', Bruno Retailleau said a primary with Glucksmann makes no sense, while David Lisnard backed it.

An Ipsos October 2026 poll gave Le Pen 33.5%, with Edouard Philippe at 15.5% as the sole Renaissance-MoDem-Horizons candidate and Mélenchon at 14%. A Toluna Harris Interactive poll the same month put Mélenchon at 17%.

On X, Laurent Marcangeli, close to Édouard Philippe, asked: "Est-on assuré que les perdants accepteraient de soutenir le vainqueur ?" Bruno Retailleau rejected the broad primary outright. "Avec Raphaël Glucksmann, la primaire n'a aucun sens. C'est le en même temps de gauche à droite, Marine Le Pen n'attend que ça !", he wrote, a ticket of left and right that would hand Le Pen exactly the contest she wants.

Unlike them, David Lisnard backed the open primary, having proposed it for two years, and urged speed: "Mais il faut se dépêcher car bientôt il sera trop tard". In the Ipsos October 2026 poll testing an Attal-Philippe scenario, 42% of Macron's 2022 voters would choose Édouard Philippe, 13% Attal and 18% Raphaël Glucksmann.

The numbers are moving Attal's way, and slightly against Le Pen. Ipsos found Attal up from 14.5% in September to a higher figure in October, while Le Pen slipped slightly from 34%. The same month, Toluna Harris Interactive polled 2,094 registered voters for M6 and RTL over October 19 to 21.
