Belgrade court detains police commander over garage torture case

Belgrade judges ordered SAJ commander Igor Žmirić held for up to 30 days with eight other suspects, while bar association president Vladimir Prijovic says the timing of arrests raises suspicion that police and prosecutors serve political ends.

6 November 2026

A Belgrade court ordered Igor Žmirić, commander of Serbia's Special Anti-Terrorist Unit (SAJ), into detention for up to 30 days on suspicion of abuse and torture. The order came two weeks after the Student List defeated the Serbian Progressive Party in the parliamentary elections. Žmirić is one of nine suspects, several of them high-ranking police officials, accused of torturing ten detainees in the Government building garage on 14-15 August 2025.

Belgrade Bar Association president Vladimir Prijovic spoke on 21 October, at the close of the campaign before the 25 October parliamentary elections. Belgrade Bar Association president Vladimir Prijovic said the timing of the arrests and searches raises suspicion police and prosecutors serve political ends. Such suspicion, he said, demands serious public attention and independent review. The Student List's candidates have demanded an investigation into whether Belgrade chief prosecutor Nenad Stefanović used his powers to favour the defendants.

Žmirić and the others, in co-perpetration with first defendant M. K., are suspected of inflicting great pain and severe suffering on the night of 14-15 August in the garage on Nemanjina Street. Serbia's Interior Ministry said the suspects, acting as members of the JZO gendarmerie units, used force and threats to obtain information and unlawfully punish the victims. Its Internal Control Sector then ordered the nine, eight serving officers and one former member, held for up to 48 hours at the request of the First Basic Public Prosecutor's Office in Belgrade.

On the order of the Higher Public Prosecutor's Office in Belgrade, police detained Srđan Ilić, an activist of the Beograd ostaje movement, in Zemun, on suspicion of organizing violence around the 25 October elections. In a coordinated action, five people were arrested in Novi Sad on suspicion of planning violence against Serbia's constitutional order and security on election day, 25 October. Beograd ostaje said it will use all legal means to defend Ilić and that intimidation will not silence citizens.

Stefanović says he will respect the Constitution and the law and expects a swift examination of the criminal complaint against him. The First Basic Court said detention was ordered so the suspects would not influence witnesses, and because they allegedly committed a violent crime punishable by more than five years in a way that caused public anxiety threatening the fair conduct of proceedings.
