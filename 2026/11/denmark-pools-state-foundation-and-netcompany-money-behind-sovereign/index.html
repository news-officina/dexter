Denmark pools state, foundation and Netcompany money behind sovereign AI supercomputer

EIFO puts DKK 150 million of government funding into DCAI, with Netcompany investing DKK 325 million, as ministers frame the deal as ownership of future technology Europe must hold itself.

14 November 2026

Netcompany has joined EIFO and the Novo Nordisk Foundation as a joint owner of the Danish Centre for AI Innovation (DCAI). EIFO provides DKK 150 million, funded by the Danish government. Netcompany is investing DKK 325 million of a DKK 500 million raise, about €67 million at the ECB euro reference rate of 10 November 2026.

The ministers framed the deal as a matter of European technological sovereignty. Business Minister Martin Lidegaard said on 10 November that Denmark must shape the technology of the future and that the investment ensures ownership of future technology is not left to others. Research Minister Christina Egelund said Europe must be able to do much more itself, not least so it can regulate and develop technology on democratic values. Once the deal closes, the Novo Nordisk Foundation will hold 52.6 percent of DCAI, EIFO and Netcompany 23.7 percent each.

DCAI operates Gefion, Denmark's first AI supercomputer. The Novo Nordisk Foundation and Denmark's state investment fund previously provided DKK 700 million, about €94 million, for the machine. Netcompany subscribed for new shares in DCAI for DKK 325 million, based on a pre-money valuation of DKK 875 million. At the ECB rate of 10 November 2026, that valuation is about €117 million.

EIFO's chief executive, Peder Lundquist, said demand for AI capacity in Denmark is expected to increase at least sixfold within five years. "AI is fundamentally changing our economy and society, and within only five years, we expect the demand for AI capacity in Denmark to increase at least sixfold. Danish access to a strong and sovereign AI infrastructure is therefore essential".

Backed by the new capital, DCAI is to build Gefion into a scalable, secure and commercially viable national AI platform. It is to serve the Danish public sector, critical industries and research institutions. Netcompany CEO André Rogaczewski will join the four-member board at completion, as part of the company's minority protection rights.
