North Korean hackers stole more than $6.3 billion from Binance, US agencies say

The FBI and Secret Service named the TraderTraitor group and urged firms to block its laundering wallets, while Pyongyang rejected the charge as absurd. The company says about $3 billion has been frozen on-chain and user funds will be covered in full.

3 November 2026

North Korean hackers stole more than $6.3 billion from Binance, the FBI and the U.S. Secret Service formally alleged on Tuesday. The theft is what Chainalysis describes as the largest cryptocurrency theft on record, surpassing the $1.5 billion Bybit hack attributed to TraderTraitor in February 2025.

The attacker still holds more than 420,000 ETH and 18,270 BTC, about $2.4 billion that lies beyond any issuer's reach.

Binance said the attacker used a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop to reach a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions. TRM Labs reported that on October 29 about $6.36 billion left Binance wallets across ten blockchains. Most of it moved in roughly 20 minutes starting at 20:05 UTC. Chainalysis said on October 30 it assessed with a high degree of confidence that TraderTraitor, a North Korean cybercrime group, carried out the theft.

Binance temporarily suspended withdrawals as an immediate precaution while completing security checks. Working with blockchain partners, it froze affected assets, with about $3 billion frozen on-chain, the company said. "Binance will cover all losses from the SAFU fund and the company's funds. User funds are safe," the company said. It will also publish an updated Proof of Reserves once remediation is complete. The exchange also said it has replenished 90% of the stolen cryptocurrencies. Withdrawals are to resume on Sunday.

The FBI, Secret Service and IRS urged industry entities, from THORNode operators to DeFi services and analytics firms, to block the TraderTraitor laundering wallets. Pyongyang dismissed the accusation. In a statement carried by KCNA, the North Korean state news agency, Kim Yo-jong called the FBI's claims absurd rhetoric bringing nothing new, citing earlier United States accusations over the Bybit hack.
