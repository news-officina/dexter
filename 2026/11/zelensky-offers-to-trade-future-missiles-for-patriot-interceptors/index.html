Zelensky offers to trade future missiles for Patriot interceptors now

German defence minister says Patriot capacity is exhausted and the EU has €21 billion in unspent defence funds that Kyiv wants put to work, as Ukraine warns that interceptors in storage will not stop Russian ballistic missiles.

18 November 2026

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky asked partners in the Ramstein format to hand over Patriot and IRIS-T interceptors now, from stockpiles, in exchange for missiles Ukraine has ordered for later delivery. The pipeline for US-made interceptors still runs through Washington's PURL programme, and Zelensky appealed to Washington not to delay it.

Ukraine has been urging its backers to find more Patriot systems, the only ones it has against Russian ballistic missiles. Boris Pistorius, the German defence minister, said his country's capacity to deliver Patriots is for now exhausted. He currently rules out further deliveries. Other states must now do more on air defence supply, he said.

The 37th meeting of the Ukraine Defence Contact Group, co-chaired at NATO headquarters in Brussels by British defence secretary Wes Streeting and Boris Pistorius, brought together almost 50 nations. Streeting told them support must run not only through the winter but through 2027. Ukraine, he said, must hold the front line and a strong position in any negotiation. The UK government announced a £240 million winter package, with more than 1,000 air-defence missiles. The UK government said deliveries start in December. The package forms part of £500 million pledged at the Budget for air defences.

Mark Rutte said Allies announced further military aid to Ukraine at the meeting, including air defence and ammunition. Germany and Norway announced a further package focused on air defence, including guided missiles for Patriot systems. Ukraine's defence ministry described Germany's part as a previously announced €1 billion package with IRIS-T systems, radars and interceptor drones; Norway is putting $300 million into PURL. The UK's package also brings more than a dozen air defence radars to identify incoming strikes.

The gap between pledges and deliveries stayed open. Zelensky urged the EU to put €21 billion in unspent Security Action for Europe funds into joint projects with Ukraine. Of the €70 billion pledged at the NATO summit in Ankara earlier in 2026, €8 billion is still undelivered, according to Radio Liberty. Zelensky called air defence Ukraine's priority. Interceptors held by the United States and European partners should be deployed in air defense batteries, he said, not kept in storage.
