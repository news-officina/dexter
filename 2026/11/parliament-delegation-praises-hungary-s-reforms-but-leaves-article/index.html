Parliament delegation praises Hungary's reforms but leaves Article 7 decision open

The delegation has no mandate to close the procedure opened in 2018, and the Council has one month to rule on lifting measures affecting 4.2 billion euros.

3 November 2026

A European Parliament civil liberties delegation welcomed Hungary's rapid rule of law reforms but took no position on closing the Article 7 procedure opened in 2018. Chair Sophie Wilmès said the contrast with the delegation's 2025 visit is striking, praising the scale and speed of reform since the new government took power.

On 23 September 2026 the Commission proposed to the Council to lift Hungary's protective measures under the conditionality regulation. The proposal would reinstate 4.2 billion euros of cohesion funding and restore full access to Erasmus and Horizon Europe for Hungarian students and researchers. Wilmès said questions remained on judicial independence, the prosecution of high-level corruption, media freedom and fundamental rights.

The delegation, however, holds no power to settle the matter. Wilmès said the group had no mandate to decide on closing the Article 7 procedure. Its task is narrower: to examine information gathered from authorities, judges, journalists and civil society.

Tisza won the April 2026 parliamentary elections, ending Orbán's 16-year rule. The new government quickly moved to amend Hungary's Constitution, as Brussels Signal reported in July. On 3 October 2026, Fidesz and KDNP MEPs wrote to Commissioner Michael McGrath. They asked the Commission to state its position on the Tisza government's measures, calling them a constitutional coup and the building of an autocratic regime. After loud Brussels rule-of-law attacks in previous years, they wrote, there was now a deafening silence from the Commission.

The decision now moves to the Council, which has one month to rule on lifting the 2022 measures. Parliament has already acted: on 8 October 2026 it adopted text T10-0342/2026 under procedure 2026/2868(RSP), in a single reading with recorded votes. The delegation's conclusions go next to LIBE, with a plenary debate set for 24 November 2026 and a resolution vote in December 2026. No final decision is confirmed.
