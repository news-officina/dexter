Iraq proposes regional ministers' committee as oil losses pass $60bn in US-Iran war

Foreign Minister Fuad Hussein says the committee would visit Tehran and Washington to seek calm. Iraq has lost $60bn, about €54 billion, since Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz, which carries 90 percent of its oil exports.

7 November 2026

On 6 November, Iraqi Foreign Minister Fuad Hussein proposed a committee of regional foreign ministers. The committee would visit both Tehran and Washington and seek peaceful understandings. Hussein warned that regional tensions were escalating and that renewed military operations remained possible. He stressed the need for practical steps to contain escalation and reduce tensions.

Iraq's oil revenue now depends on a waterway Tehran closed. Since the US war on Iran began in February 2026, Iraq has lost more than $60bn in oil revenues, about €54 billion at the ECB euro reference rate. The strait is Hormuz, through which about 90 percent of Iraq's oil exports normally pass and which Tehran shut when the war started. Oil accounts for about 90 percent of Iraq's federal budget.

Hussein's candour followed a direct American strike on Iranian territory. American forces struck Iran's Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on the eve of the 3 November midterm elections. Hussein told Dijlah TV that further US attacks on Iran are "not a secret". Hussein also warned Iraqi armed groups against joining any Iranian retaliation.

Tehran has its own terms, and Baghdad heard them directly. Araghchi told Hussein on 6 November that talks with Washington could resume on Iran's terms. These included timelines, reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a return to the Pakistan understandings and added points on the nuclear file. Hussein warned Iran-aligned armed groups could be drawn into another confrontation, exposing Iraq to US strikes.

What happens next runs through 90 days. The US-led Coalition ended its military mission in Iraq on September 30. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi then opened a 90-day de-escalation period, under which the Iran-aligned factions halt attacks and receive guarantees that US forces will not strike them. This is the first stage of a plan to bring their weapons under state control by June 30, 2027. Baghdad's window runs through those 90 days.
