Nvidia board opens inquiry into whether Huang was targeted by foreign intelligence

The board says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. Three House chairmen have demanded a closed-door briefing on Chinese access to US chip technology by November 18.

9 November 2026

A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside counsel, will oversee it, and the board said it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. That day, Huang admitted in a letter to employees he had been unfaithful to his wife.

In 2025, Jensen Huang persuaded Trump to revoke a ban on selling AI chips to China, which allowed Nvidia to sell a lower-performance chip there. He then reached an agreement with Trump to sell the company's second-strongest chip, with the US government taking 25% of sales. Trump later considered, but abandoned, allowing sales of the latest Blackwell chip, according to reports by the New York Times and UDN.

Megaspeed, an Nvidia-linked operator, has been building data centres in Southeast Asia and renting computing power to Alibaba, the New York Times reported. That gives China indirect access to advanced Nvidia chips the United States restricts from direct sale to China. The operator is under US investigation for suspected smuggling of Nvidia chips into China; Nvidia said its internal probe found no evidence of wrongdoing by Megaspeed. Bloomberg reported that Nvidia asked the government whether it should cut ties with Megaspeed, and was told it did not need to.

A memo circulated this spring among senior US policy officials in the Trump administration, the New York Times and UDN reported. It cited intelligence that a Chinese businesswoman close to Huang was a spy for China's Ministry of State Security, according to people briefed on the intelligence. On November 5, Brian Mast, chairman of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, John Moolenaar, chairman of the Select Committee on China, and Rick Crawford, chairman of the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence, sent Huang a letter asking him to appear for a joint, closed-door briefing. Recent reports, they wrote, raise significant national security concerns regarding the PRC's access to sensitive US technology and information.

Huang has until November 11 to accept the November 18, 2026 briefing date proposed by the three chairmen or propose an alternative session. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said on November 9 that the claims of a Chinese spy are pure speculation and slander.
