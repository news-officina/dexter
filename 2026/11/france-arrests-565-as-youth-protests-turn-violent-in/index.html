France arrests 565 as youth protests turn violent in Germany and France

German organisers said 120,000 joined demos, below the 180,000 expected, after Frankfurt and Berlin saw riots, looting and injured police. Ministers blame extremist infiltrators; the movement disowns them.

1 November 2026

France arrested 565 people on Saturday night as a third round of demonstrations met riot police across the country, according to Ouest-France. In Germany, violence broke out the same evening in Berlin, Cologne and Hamburg after groups of young people refused to disperse, AFP reported.

Around 120,000 people joined the German demonstrations, below the roughly 180,000 expected, the 'GenZ regelt!' movement said. A spokesman of the initiative, giving only his first name Amadeo, said more than 100,000 had taken part. The movement demands more money for schools, addiction help and psychotherapy, measures against rising living costs, social justice and more climate protection. It also rejects conscription and expanded state surveillance.

As darkness approached on Saturday, police in several cities fired teargas and stun grenades at crowds that included groups of masked rioters, the Guardian reported. In Paris a molotov cocktail was thrown at a mobile gendarme. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez called the attack attempted murder, saying the gendarme had not stopped his service.

Frankfurt's protest at the Konstablerwache escalated over hours into what police called "Violent riots", with shop windows smashed, molotov cocktails and bottles thrown at emergency personnel, one person injured, several arrests, and water cannons and armoured vehicles deployed, according to AFP and the Kölner Stadt-Anzeige. Looters struck shops in the city centre, including a gun store in the Hasengasse, from which police seized two weapons after arresting suspects, per AFP and the Kölner Stadt-Anzeige. In Berlin, police reported 27 officers slightly injured and up to 56 temporary arrests at a march shaped by left-radical groups, n-tv and the Kölner Stadt-Anzeige reported.

Hesse's interior minister Roman Poseck blamed around 100 clearly identified left-wing extremists for infiltrating the Frankfurt protest and massively attacking the police, according to AFP and the Kölner Stadt-Anzeige. Extremist groups, anti-constitutional symbols, racism and hate speech have no place with it, its organisers said.

The state blames a hundred; the movement disowns them.
