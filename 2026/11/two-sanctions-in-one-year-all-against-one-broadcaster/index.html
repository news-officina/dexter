Two sanctions in one year, all against one broadcaster, are politics

The Media Authority warned, fined and threatened Ongehoord Nederland for a year before acting. When Zembla and BNNVARA broke the rules, it stayed silent, and that is what destroys a regulator.

2 November 2026

Ongehoord Nederland may now be thrown out of the public broadcasting system, and the strangest part is not why the minister wants it gone. It is how long the system took to reach that point. The Media Authority called the broadcast of a Hitler fragment a tipping point, one that enables the minister to act, in its judgement reported on 17 September 2026. A tipping point implies there was something to tip. The question is what the Authority was waiting on before that day.

What it was waiting on is on the record. On 8 September 2026, Minister Letschert moved to withdraw Ongehoord Nederland's recognition, saying there are clear rules for broadcasters that make programmes with public money, and that repeatedly breaking them means it ends. She concluded ON! no longer complies with the legal rules for the public broadcaster. The minister was right to conclude it. Within a single year it had fined ON! once and imposed a penalty payment once, over conflicts of interest, the redactiestatuut, the rules for editorial independence, and its financial administration. Every month of that year was a month the law sat unenforced.

The record shows how slowly. Warned, fined and threatened, and still the organisation faced removal only when in September the Authority judged the fragment "een kantelpunt", a tipping point that enables the minister to act. And the machinery is not merely slow. It is selective. When the investigative programme Zembla fabricated tweets, the programme apologised and the episode ended there. In September, the Media Authority had imposed no fine on the broadcaster BNNVARA for it, and the NPO ombudsman had conducted no investigation. A committee led by Van Rijn had found that BNNVARA did not properly handle reports about abuses around its programme De Wereld Draait Door. No fine, no investigation. Sanction for one broadcaster, silence for another. That is not enforcement. It is selection.

The strongest defence of the Media Authority is this: in the September report in which the Authority called the fragment "een kantelpunt", a regulator that had already moved step by step, two sanctions in one year, reached the moment that proved ON! cannot be fixed. Zembla, by contrast, ended in apology. Unequal outcomes can reflect unequal facts, not unequal politics.

That argument holds for the timing, not for the pattern. We do not ask the Media Authority to treat like as unlike. One book for everyone, kept in the open: that is all we ask. Public money with rules applied by taste is not a system, it is preference, and a regulator that prefers destroys the authority it is meant to protect. So Parliament must require the Authority to publish, once a year and before the year closes, every complaint, investigation and sanction opened against every public broadcaster. The record of 8 September says ON!, after the definitive decision, can still go to court in bezwaar or beroep, the Dutch routes of administrative objection and appeal. The minister must confirm in writing that every broadcaster in the system is held to one standard, whatever voice it carries, and that ON! keeps that exact due process any other would get. Fix the book, or lose the rules.
