Kallas says Europe will refuse to implement any Ukraine deal it did not join

Washington and Moscow keep negotiating in formats with no seat for Europe, while Meloni asks the December summit to pick a single negotiator. Kallas said any agreement must stop Russia using a pause to rearm.

7 November 2026

Europe cannot be left out of any deal on Ukraine, and will refuse to implement one it did not join, EU High Representative Kaja Kallas said. Donald Trump, after meeting Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev on November 5, said a "big, beautiful agreement" could involve more than Ukraine, possibly all of Europe.

Washington negotiated with Russia in Abu Dhabi on a trilateral format that left no seat for Europe. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said on November 3 that the trilateral format had been coordinated. Russia must eventually discuss European security with them, he said. Jared Kushner told European partners the United States meant only to consult them on the sidelines, then possibly widen the process. Ukraine's foreign minister Andriy Sybiha, after meeting Kaja Kallas in Warsaw, said neither Ukraine's future nor Europe's security could be decided without both at the table.

Kallas argued that the problem is not a missing structure but a broken one. Europe already has a security architecture in place, she said in Warsaw; Russia keeps breaking the treaties under it. NATO posed no threat to Russia in 2021 and poses none now, she said, so Putin has no veto over European security. Radoslaw Sikorski, Poland's foreign minister, said an arrangement on Ukraine's and Europe's future made above Europeans' heads would be unwise and impossible.

Kallas set Europe's condition: any agreement must ensure Russia does not attack again, and must stop Moscow using any pause to rearm for a new offensive. Peskov said on November 3 that indivisible security is a cornerstone of the Kremlin's position and that guarantees can only be mutual.

Italy's Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni has written to European Council President Antonio Costa that Europe must speak with one voice on Ukraine. She wants the December summit to decide on a single negotiator. Kaja Kallas has set out why that voice matters: the EU is indispensable to security guarantees, post-war reconstruction and sanctions.
