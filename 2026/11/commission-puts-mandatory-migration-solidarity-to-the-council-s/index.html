Commission puts mandatory migration solidarity to the Council's decision

The Solidarity Pool for 2027 would let member states pay €20,000 per person not relocated, but contributions bind only once the Council agrees, and Hungary and Slovakia pledged nothing for 2026.

6 November 2026

The European Commission launched the second Annual Migration Management Cycle on 6 November 2026 and proposed the Solidarity Pool for 2027. It found Greece, Cyprus, Italy and Spain under migratory pressure. It cited disproportionate arrivals, including rescue operations at sea for Italy and the situation in Ceuta for Spain.

The stakes are whether the solidarity rule binds. The Commission stated that Member States' solidarity contributions become legally binding only once the Council adopts its decision, and that they will then be made public. The same Council decision will set the pool's size and how each Member State contributes.

The Commission identified fifteen member states as at risk of migratory pressure, from Belgium and Germany to Finland. It gave them priority access to the Permanent EU Migration Support Toolbox. At the High-Level Solidarity Forum, it also tabled an implementing decision under Article 11 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1351, followed by an exchange of views.

The Commission's evidence for this is the 2026 Asylum and Migration Report, published the same day with its fourth assessment of the Pact's state of play. It showed illegal border crossings down by more than a third in the first half of 2026. They had fallen by more than half over the previous two years. Asylum applications fell by 19% in 2025, unauthorised movements by 22%, and the EU-wide return rate passed 30%. Further effort is needed to make all Pact components fully operational, it said, in particular the responsibility rules and transfers back of applicants. It will raise shortcomings with the High-Level Solidarity Forum and, if they persist, with the Council.

Brunner defends the Pact

Magnus Brunner, the European Commissioner for Migration, presented the numbers as proof the new approach works. Speaking of Italy, he said, "Irregular arrivals have fallen by over 55 per cent." By his account, the Western Balkans route is down 95 per cent in recent years, and the Central Mediterranean route by 80 per cent. "The system is mandatory", an EU official said of Hungary and Slovakia, which did not pledge for the previous year. The Commission is in contact with both, the official said, "to see how we can get respect of this dimension".

The decision now sits with the member state governments. Under the Commission's proposal, each contributor may choose between relocating asylum seekers, paying €20,000 for each person not relocated, or funding operational support in countries under pressure.

The 2026 Annual Asylum and Migration Report found Hungary and Slovakia in breach for pledging no relocations or financial contributions for 2026, according to Euronews. The Commission will launch no infringement procedures for now. Eunews, citing a European source, reports another gap: solidarity need not be activated for a state the Commission concludes has systemic shortcomings under the regulation.

Choices and shortfalls

The Pact's solidarity framework allows each member state to choose how it contributes: relocation of asylum seekers, financial support, responsibility offsets, alternative measures, or a combination. Euronews reports fewer than 9,000 effective relocations in 2026 against the watered-down reference figure of 21,000.

A European source, cited by Eunews, said Italy certainly has access to the solidarity mechanism. The source said the situation must be monitored and taken into account in implementing the mechanism in the coming months. Eurodac data reported by Euronews suggest unauthorised movements within the EU fell by around 13%. Some 13,000 transfers back between member states were carried out in the reporting period. The next test of the Commission's operational capacity came on 27 October 2026, when it tabled a recast European Border and Coast Guard proposal.
