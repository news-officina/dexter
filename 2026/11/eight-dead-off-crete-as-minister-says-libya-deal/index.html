At least 20 dead off Crete as minister says Libya deal no longer holds

Shipping Minister Sevi Voloudaki says cooperation with eastern Libya has broken down and warns of stricter measures if the EU's return-hub initiative fails within days. Frontex aircraft spotted two further boats south of Crete, with roughly 47 and approximately 40 people aboard.

9 November 2026

Correction: An earlier version left out a second boat, found off Crete early on Monday 9 November with 12 dead and 18 survivors, and placed Monday's details with the boat found on Saturday.

The Greek coastguard recovered 12 bodies and rescued 18 people from a dinghy off southern Crete in the early hours of Monday, 9 November, after a Moroccan-flagged container ship spotted it 15 nautical miles south-east of Kali Limenes. On Saturday evening it had recovered eight bodies from another boat and rescued 28 refugees and migrants, with two people still missing. According to those survivors, two more people had fallen into the sea.

Shipping Minister Sevi Voloudaki linked the rise in crossings to a broken arrangement with eastern Libya. In recent days, she said, the cooperation no longer holds. Greece is not handling this alone. She said work continues at diplomatic and bilateral level, in cooperation with the European Union and the European Commission, to find a solution.

The coastguard was first informed of the Saturday boat on Friday afternoon, when a dinghy with foreigners aboard was reported in distress six nautical miles south-southwest of Kali Limenes. Heraklion's Central Coast Guard Authority opened a preliminary investigation. After survivors identified them, it arrested two Sudanese nationals, aged 28 and 16, as the suspected smugglers. The coastguard account was carried by Ta Nea and Brussels Times. A case file was opened against both for illegal entry, facilitating illegal entry, forming a gang and fatal exposure.

Greece's Joint Rescue Coordination Centre ran two further search and rescue operations south of Crete in the same Monday hours. In the first, a Frontex aircraft spotted a boat carrying roughly 47 migrants 30 nautical miles south of Kali Limenes. In the second, a Frontex unmanned aerial vehicle detected another boat with approximately 40 people aboard, 31 nautical miles south-east of the same cape. Frontex rescue teams picked up the people on both boats.

Sevi Voloudaki said that minister Pleuris will announce within days a new initiative, in cooperation with other EU member states, for so-called return hubs in a country outside Europe's territory. If that effort fails within a short time, she warned, much stricter measures will follow, either the ones used last summer in similar cases or others.
