Russia quits Europe's torture prevention treaty, ending detention monitoring in Russia

The withdrawal took effect one year after notification, and no independent body can now inspect Russian places of detention. The Council of Europe says the CPT will continue its work in the 46 remaining member states.

2 November 2026

Russia's withdrawal from the European Convention for the Prevention of Torture took effect on 1 November 2026. CPT President Alan Mitchell said the move ends the Committee's monitoring mandate and any independent monitoring of places of deprivation of liberty inside Russia. He added that the Convention's preventive approach remains as important as ever.

The Council of Europe reports that Ukraine lets the CPT visit any place of detention at any time and publishes the reports automatically. The EU's external action service notes that Russia has allowed no monitoring visits to places of detention.

The road out began in September 2025, when Vladimir Putin signed a federal law denouncing the Convention and its Protocols No. 1 and 2. Under Article 22 of the Convention, the Council of Europe holds that withdrawal takes effect one year after notification, which puts the date in 2026. Russia says it has had no seat on the Committee since December 2023, after the Council of Europe blocked the election of a new Russian member.

Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, the CPT has received numerous accounts of alleged torture and ill-treatment by Russian officials, in occupied Ukrainian territories and within Russia. It could not visit Russia, because, as the Council of Europe reports, the Russian authorities did not cooperate. On 18 November 2024 the Committee put the record in public, in a public statement concerning the Russian Federation.

The EU's external action service called the decision one more step in Russia's complete disengagement from its international commitments and said it demonstrates Russia's disregard for the protection of human rights. The CPT, for its part, will continue its mandate in the 46 remaining member states of the Council of Europe.
