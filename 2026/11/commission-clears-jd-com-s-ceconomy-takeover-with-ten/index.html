Commission clears JD.com's Ceconomy takeover with ten-year market-terms commitments

The approval closes the final regulatory hurdle. In August 2026 Beijing barred organisations and individuals from cooperating with the EU subsidy probe and later warned of countermeasures.

3 November 2026

The European Commission approved JD.com's acquisition of Ceconomy under the Foreign Subsidies Regulation, conditional on commitments that intra-group transactions, including financing, run on market terms. The commitments last ten years, with an independent trustee monitoring implementation under Commission supervision. Ceconomy called the clearance the final outstanding regulatory approval. With all offer conditions fulfilled, Ceconomy and JD.com said in early November 2026 that they would close the transaction within days.

In August 2026, China barred organisations and individuals from complying with or assisting the EU's foreign subsidy probe into JD.com, calling the measures improper extraterritorial jurisdiction.

The Commission's case rested on what Beijing had given JD.com. It found that preferential financing, tax incentives and grants from the People's Republic were liable to distort the internal market, and demanded a remedy.

The Commission sent the initial commitments to a market test, in which competitors identified shortcomings in them. In response, the parties strengthened the intra-group financing commitments and improved the terms for third-party access to logistics and technology services. China's justice ministry warned: "If the EU persists in its approach, China will firmly take countermeasures in accordance with the law".

The buyout began in December 2025, when 290,228,196 shares were tendered, 59.8 percent of capital and voting rights, 85.2 percent counting Convergenta's 25.35 percent stake, and the approvals in this article clear the way for its closing within days.
