Hoax bomb threats ground or divert at least seven planes across Europe in two days

Airlines hit include LOT, Scandinavian Airlines and Wizz Air, and the AP and Telegraph report one aircraft was escorted by the Italian Air Force. Osprey Flight Solutions says the hoaxes fit a pattern of hybrid attacks on critical infrastructure.

5 November 2026

Hoax bomb threats sent by email grounded or diverted at least seven planes across Europe on 3 November 2026, three of them British. Bulgarian interior ministry official Lyubomir Nikolov said the senders had started targeting certain airlines, "forcing aircraft to change course". Both aircraft hit that day were searched and nothing suspicious was found.

The series was not a single day's disruption. On 2 November, a Wizz Air flight from London Luton to Sofia was diverted to Budapest after a bomb threat was received during the flight. The other came on 3 November, when a London Heathrow to Warsaw Chopin flight received a bomb alarm and landed at Warsaw-Modlin Airport at 10:01.

Aircraft operated by LOT, Scandinavian Airlines and Wizz Air were among those hit, according to the Associated Press and the Telegraph. One of them, the report said, was escorted by the Italian Air Force.

Efforts to identify the sender were continuing, Bulgarian National Radio reported.

Osprey Flight Solutions' head said the hoaxes would fit a pattern of hybrid warfare against critical infrastructure, suggesting retaliation for Ukrainian attacks on Russian air travel. Estonia has already seen a parallel attack: hackers, per public broadcaster ERR, raised supermarket refrigeration units to 50C.
