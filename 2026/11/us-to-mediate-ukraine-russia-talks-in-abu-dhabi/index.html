US to mediate Ukraine-Russia talks in Abu Dhabi, Kremlin keeps Europe out

The Kremlin says the trilateral format bars European participation, while Zelensky insists Europe must be included. Brussels will host separate recovery talks a week earlier.

4 November 2026

The United States will mediate working-level talks between Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November, US Special Envoy Steve Witkoff announced. The Kremlin said European representatives cannot take part because this is an agreed trilateral format of Russia, Ukraine and the US.

Witkoff said the Abu Dhabi discussions would focus on "post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian efforts, and next steps toward peace." Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine insists on Europe's participation in the process, while European governments are unlikely to be at the table.

The US delegation is expected to include Witkoff and Jared Kushner, according to Reuters, citing a source. Russia will send a delegation led by presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed, including representatives from several agencies, among them the foreign ministry. The exact composition, Peskov said, awaits the president's approval.

The Commission confirmed on 3 November that it will host talks in Brussels on 4 and 5 November with US, Ukrainian and European representatives on Ukraine's post-war recovery. Officials from France, Germany, the UK, NATO and the World Bank will attend. The Commission described the gathering as a continuation of October talks in Miami, Florida and Oslo with largely the same participants. The Commission said that at working level it would examine in detail the developed proposals on postwar recovery and economic development.

Zelensky said the most sensitive issues, including territorial ones, should be discussed at leaders' level. "We are counting on the fact that work in this format and our other diplomatic steps will open the way to a trilateral format at the level of leaders," he said. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said he does not yet see Russia seriously disposed to negotiate peace, and that negotiations must lead to reconciliation and a ceasefire.
