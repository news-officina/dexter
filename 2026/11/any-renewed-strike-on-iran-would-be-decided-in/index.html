Any renewed strike on Iran would be decided in Washington, and Europe holds no seat there

Whether, when and against what any renewed American strike on Iran happens turns on one man in Washington; Israel's pressing for it is reported and denied, Qatar mediates and Iran waits. No European capital or institution appears at any point of the chain that reports show deciding the question.

2 November 2026

Tucker Carlson posted on X on November 1 that the Trump administration "may be within days of launching a nuclear strike on Iran". He attributed the pressure to Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The target he named was Pickaxe Mountain, a deep underground site near Natanz in central Iran.

The claim rests on one unnamed "informed observer", quoted as saying "Bibi calls Trump about it every day". The observer added "I wouldn't use nuclear. We don't have to". No documents, named sources or independent evidence have been presented. Nothing in the post goes beyond that single anonymous account.

The post had drawn more than 48 million views by Monday.

The question now is whether the United States launches a renewed strike on Iran, and on whom that decision turns.

The War Zone reported on October 27, in a disputed account, that three carrier strike groups and two amphibious ready groups would reach CENTCOM this November.

The Middle East Forum judged otherwise. In its reading, reinforcements remain at sea and forces have been repositioned, which contradicts a strike within days from full readiness.

Carlson's earlier reporting

In March, Carlson reported that Israel had demanded the American strikes on Iran of June 2025. Trump said the decision was his own and that he might have forced Israel's hand. Secretary of State Marco Rubio attributed the timing to the president's own analysis.

Carlson's claims that Hegseth, Rubio and Ratcliffe back Netanyahu's push, and that American doctrine was rewritten to ease first use, remain unconfirmed.

The men who would make the decision say otherwise. Trump said in a Newsmax interview in October, "No, I wouldn't use nuclear. We don't have to", and called it "just an extremely large bomb". Rubio says no decision on fresh strikes has been made and that diplomacy is preferred.

Carlson said "no conventional weapon in the American arsenal can penetrate 300 feet of solid granite". CSIS reports the 30,000-pound MOP penetrates up to 200 feet, with remaining stock estimated in the low single digits. The Institute for Science and International Security, based on October satellite imagery, found Pickaxe Mountain's entrances obstructed and assessed the site as not operational.

Posture for a campaign

Set the unconfirmed claim aside and a slower fork stands on record, and every strand of it runs through Washington. The Middle East Forum judged, with moderate confidence, that the United States is postured for a renewed campaign against Iran after November 3 if the president orders one.

The Wall Street Journal, citing two officials, reported a Patriot battery moving to Israel, troops moved off vulnerable bases and munitions restocked. That report is disputed. A White House official, asked about military plans, said they "may or may not exist".

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said talks with Iran "never stopped". Iranian negotiators spent a third day in Doha over reopening the Strait of Hormuz within two days of a deal. Trump told reporters at Mar-a-Lago that negotiations were going fine, and that he was "not in much of a hurry".

Iran has warned that any attack on Pickaxe Mountain would prompt a strong response.

Trump names the target

Trump named the target on the record. On the Hugh Hewitt show in July, he said, "We are going to take out Pickaxe Mountain." In the same remarks he said, "We're watching Pickaxe Mountain very closely. We don't see any activity there."

Carlson's account of motive is specific. According to Tampa Free Press, a Florida-based news website, he ties the daily lobbying to Netanyahu's politics. The Israeli election left Netanyahu short of the 61 Knesset seats needed to form a coalition, and the strike serves survival.

Tzipi Hotovely, head of the National Public Diplomacy Directorate, said, "The issue of Pickaxe Mountain was not raised in the Netanyahu-Trump meeting." She added, "Reports claiming that Israel came with intelligence information are fake."

The weekend arguments

The two arguments that matter both come from the same weekend. Netanyahu pressed for a strike after the midterms while Trump wanted talks to run, in a call Axios called tough. Israel's ambassador Danny Danon answered that "Prime Minister Netanyahu is not pressing President Trump". Iran holds its warning, Qatar holds the talks, Washington holds the trigger.
