Supermarket refrigeration hacked again across Europe three weeks after France

The Commissioner called the repeat attacks a deliberate attempt to disrupt the food supply. The vulnerabilities were flagged before the first attack.

3 November 2026

Hackers again switched off refrigeration systems at supermarkets across Europe on Monday 2 November 2026, three weeks after similar attacks in France. Agriculture and Food Commissioner Christophe Hansen called the repetition extremely concerning. He said it was a deliberate attempt to disrupt the food supply chain and undermine consumer confidence, a warning to be taken seriously.

CISA, the United States critical infrastructure authority, published an advisory on these same vulnerabilities in August 2025. In Europe, the French cybersecurity agency CERT-FR alerted member states about internet-exposed controllers of this type on 12 October 2026. Two days later, the British firm Pen Test Partners showed that the controllers' access code was generated from the day's date alone, with no device-specific secret.

At least 17 smaller shops or companies in the Netherlands were affected, Sic Notícias reported. In Estonia, attackers accessed a computer controlling a supermarket's refrigeration system and raised the temperature to 50°C. Kitman Coldtech's Jann Mutso said it was dealt with within minutes with no significant damage, and Selver confirmed one outlet affected.

Hansen said Europe's food security is not threatened at present, but the food waste and its consequences for businesses are very real. A Commission spokesperson said the EU has various tools at its disposal to manage such incidents and will keep working with member states on cyberattacks. The spokesperson said the attacks show that cyberattacks can disrupt services, strike companies and touch citizens' daily lives directly.

What caused the failures is not settled. Quintin, serving in the federal prosecutor's office, said the exact causes remain to be established, no scenario is excluded, not even a cyberattack, and an investigation into the incidents has been opened. Quintin said the incidents are limited to the cooling installations of certain shops, and stressed computer systems must be better protected and the sector more resilient to cyber threats.
