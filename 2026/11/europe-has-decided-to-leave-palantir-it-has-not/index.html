A sovereign Europe, argued plainly.

Europe has decided to leave Palantir. It has not yet built what comes next.

Spain, the United Kingdom and Germany have moved to end their dependence on Palantir, while NATO still runs the Palantir-derived system it chose when no European alternative existed. The Bundeswehr is testing about thirty European candidates, but no replacement is in service: sovereignty on this layer is a decision taken, not a capacity confirmed.

13 November 2026

On 11 November 2026, Helsing unveiled Clarity, an agentic intelligence platform for military intelligence built in Europe. At the launch, Gundbert Scherf, the company's co-CEO and co-founder, declared: "A capability this critical must be sovereign." He said European armed forces with Clarity get software developed in Europe, running on their own infrastructure and staying under their control.

The record behind that sentence is harder. NATO bought its Maven Smart System, derived from software the Pentagon entrusted to Palantir, for lack of a European alternative.

Thomas Daum, head of the Bundeswehr's cyber troops, told Handelsblatt that Maven is "not an option" for Germany at the moment. "I do not see that at all at the moment", he said, as quoted in German.

The stakes are concrete. Scherf told Süddeutsche Zeitung such a system is essential for Germany's defence capability. „Was bringen uns Terrabytes an Daten, wenn wir Stunden brauchen, um sie auszuwerten?“, he asked, as quoted in German by Süddeutsche Zeitung.

Francesca Bria, EuroStack Project director, told El País it is dangerous that Palantir's software, at the heart of European society, processes the data of millions of Europeans.

In early October 2026, the United Kingdom decided to end or replace its relationship with Palantir, according to El País.

In 2025, Germany excluded Palantir from a major Bundeswehr military-cloud project, and its domestic intelligence service chose ChapsVision instead, Euronomy reported in July.

"It is unthinkable that employees of the company would have access to military data", said Thomas Daum, head of the Bundeswehr's cyber troops, as quoted in German by Süddeutsche Zeitung.

The business being unwound is worth about €264 million across EU states and institutions, and more than €1.4 billion including the United Kingdom.

Palantir chief executive Alex Karp told shareholders on 3 August that the company's performance in Europe was poor: "The growth sucks".

Bundeswehr tests thirty systems

The Bundeswehr wants to buy such a system and is testing about thirty solutions from European companies, including Helsing's, Süddeutsche Zeitung reported.

Helsing says sovereignty is built into Clarity: the data, the models and the operations stay under the customer's control. The platform runs on infrastructure the customer owns. Clarity processes data from every source available to a unit, cross-checks them and analyses large volumes in seconds, the company says.

The company says an operator chooses targets and must authorise every transfer to a weapon system. A target is proposed only when independent sources agree. Every decision is documented with the name of the responsible person.

In September 2026, Thales launched Hexaforce, a "souverain" military command system augmented by AI, according to the company's description.

According to reports cited by The Decoder, in the Iran war the US military used Palantir's Maven with Anthropic's Claude. It proposed and prioritised hundreds of targets and recommended armament. The US struck around 1,000 targets in the first 24 hours and more than 3,000 within days, the Wall Street Journal reported.

Palantir points to the record

A Palantir spokesman pointed to the record, according to FAZ. The software has proven itself on the battlefields in Ukraine and as the operating system of NATO and the world's leading military power.

No one, not even Palantir itself, can access a customer's data without that customer's involvement, the spokesman said.

A Palantir spokesman did not dispute that Europe should build its own industry. European states would be right to support the industrial development of their own competitors in this field over the coming 15 years, he told FAZ.

Mixing this with the debate on sovereignty risks Europe not being prepared for the national security threats the continent is confronted with in the next five years, a Palantir spokesman said, as quoted in German by FAZ.

Customers hold the decision

The decision to move away from Palantir belongs to the customer states: Spain, the United Kingdom, and Germany through exclusion and procurement. The alliance through which defence is organised, meanwhile, still runs the Palantir-derived system. The vendor has itself logged the loss of that business.

The capacity to run what comes next sits in trial, not in service. No confirmed result. And speed carries its own risk: a chatbot's false claim almost brought the US military to board a Chinese ship in spring 2026. "AI makes it possible to get to a bad idea faster", a source told CNN, as quoted in German.

Europe holds the decision to leave Palantir.

The Bundeswehr's own prototype falls due by mid-2027.
