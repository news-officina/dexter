Commission opens consultation on draft rule for AI Act penalty proceedings

The four-week consultation on the 10-page draft closes on 9 November 2026, with fines of up to 35 million euros or 7% of worldwide turnover at stake. Eight organisations have filed submissions so far.

1 November 2026

The European Commission opened a four-week consultation on 12 October 2026 on a 10-page draft implementing regulation, Ares(2026)9589403, on proceedings under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Fines can reach 35 million euros or 7% of worldwide turnover. The draft states it has not been adopted and may not be regarded as an official Commission position. Consultation closes 9 November 2026.

Narracomm holds that the draft's structure concentrates file-handling and timing control with the Commission for systems under Article 75(1) exclusive competence. On that reading, the question is who controls file access and timing, not how much power the Commission carries. The draft text itself specifies no adoption timing, and, as noted, it is not an adopted or official Commission position.

It lays down arrangements for operators' access to the file and negotiated disclosure under Article 75d(2) and (3), and sets limitation periods for penalties and enforcement under Article 75c. The consultation opened on 12 October as a public 'Have your say' process.

GISAD has argued that the AI Office investigates, decides and imposes fines without functional separation, and recovers its own investigation costs from the operator. The draft also sets six investigative steps, from a request for information to a structured dialogue, GISAD says. According to ppc.land, Article 75c(8) sets a five-year limitation period, which each step would reset, up to a ten-year cap. GISAD adds that suspensions can push the period beyond even that.

Eight organisations have filed submissions so far, one each.
