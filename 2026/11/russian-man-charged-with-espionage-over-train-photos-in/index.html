Russian man charged with espionage over train photos in Świdnica

Prosecutors found several hundred thousand files of railway and telecoms infrastructure on the man's devices, in a case officials link to wider Russian sabotage. In Warsaw, the president cut short a foreign trip after riot police blocked the Constitutional Tribunal.

1 November 2026

A Polish prosecutor has charged a Russian man with espionage for foreign intelligence against Poland under Article 130 § 1 of the Penal Code. The District Court in Świdnica ordered three months of pre-trial detention on 28 October 2026 at the prosecutor's request. The man was arrested after taking pictures of passing trains at an unguarded crossing in Świdnica.

The case sits inside a wider pattern. European officials point to an escalation of Russian sabotage attacks in recent months, according to Politico. Putin seeks to disrupt military support for Ukraine and raise the political cost of backing Kyiv. After attacks in September 2026, Donald Tusk said Russia was already waging a hybrid war against Poland, adding: "Russia does not even have to cross our border".

The man, a 55-year-old Russian citizen, was detained by police in Świdnica in the Lower Silesian Voivodeship in October 2026. Several hundred thousand files were found on the secured devices, including photos mainly of railway tracks, trains, stations and telecommunications infrastructure in Poland and abroad. The case will be taken over by the Lower Silesian Branch Office of the National Prosecutor's Office's Department for Organized Crime and Corruption in Wrocław.

In Russia, railway enthusiasts tell a simpler story. According to RIA Novosti, enthusiasts identified him at once as a spotter whose profiles carry many transport photographs from Germany, Russia and other countries. Riot police were deployed outside the Constitutional Tribunal in Warsaw on Friday and blocked access to the building, according to Al Jazeera and the Associated Press.

President Karol Nawrocki abandoned his Senegal trip and flew back to Warsaw on Friday 30 October, his plane turning back, reportedly over Spain, before about 10 p.m., according to Al Jazeera and the Associated Press. On the night of 31 October he stated that the constitutional order had been violated and a constitutional organ of state forcibly seized. He called for the legal order to be restored and for the government's coercion forces to leave the Tribunal's premises.
