Meta, Google and TikTok sue to block California's ban on addictive feeds for minors

The companies filed separate First Amendment challenges to Assembly Bill 1709 and asked a federal court to stop Attorney General Rob Bonta enforcing the law, which takes effect in January 2027.

6 November 2026

Meta, Google and TikTok asked a federal court on November 4 to strike down California's ban on addictive feeds for under-16s before January 1, 2027. The companies filed separate suits against Assembly Bill 1709, arguing the restriction violates the First Amendment. Each seeks to block Attorney General Rob Bonta from enforcing it.

The California suits follow a run of defeats for the industry elsewhere. In June, the Sixth Circuit refused the tech industry group NetChoice an injunction against Ohio's law limiting minors' access to social media. The majority held that the companies' interests conflict with those of their minor users and that they cannot assert their users' First Amendment rights. October brought two Eleventh Circuit decisions rejecting NetChoice's challenge to a Georgia law and a second suit by the Computer & Communications Industry Association, another industry group, against a Florida law.

Meta's suit rests on three legal claims. It says Assembly Bill 1709 is preempted by Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act. The company argues California cannot "appoint the state as the editor, curator, and controller of online speech". It argues feed content receives First Amendment protection under Moody v. NetChoice, the 2024 ruling on such state laws, because ordering and display are Meta's own expressive choices. Because the ban restricts speech by content and by speaker, Meta says strict scrutiny applies, and that the law would fail even the lower intermediate scrutiny.

Meta filed its complaint for declaratory and injunctive relief in the Northern District of California, naming Bonta in his official capacity. In August, Judge Edward Davila denied the companies a preliminary injunction against Senate Bill 976 and refused to keep it paused during a Ninth Circuit appeal. The Electronic Privacy Information Center says the new suits mostly rehash arguments a district court rejected then.

Bonta's office said its staff are reviewing the suits and remain committed to defending California's laws protecting children online. Assembly Bill 1709 takes effect on January 1, 2027.
