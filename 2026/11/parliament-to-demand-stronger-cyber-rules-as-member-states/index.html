Parliament to demand stronger cyber rules as Member States contest the Commission's Act

The 11 November resolution presses for resilience of critical infrastructure while the Council contests ENISA's role, definitions and high-risk supplier rules.

3 November 2026

The European Parliament debates on Wednesday 11 November at 15:00 in Brussels a resolution demanding stronger cyber resilience of Europe's critical infrastructure and essential supply chains. The motion, 2026/2948(RSP), follows the Commission's January 2026 cybersecurity package and a revised Cybersecurity Act that Member States, per European Parliament documents, still contest in Council.

The backdrop is the package the Commission proposed on 20 January 2026. It set out to enhance the security of the EU's ICT supply chains. The Cybersecurity Act 2 file was assigned to the ITRE committee, with Marketa Gregorova (Greens/EFA, CZ) appointed rapporteur.

The Commission says the proposal will ensure that products reaching EU citizens are cyber-secure by design through a simpler certification process. Member States raised issues with the Act, according to the European Parliament, on ENISA's increased operational role, the clarity of definitions, voting rules, resources and participation in certification.

Member States also raised concerns about the Commission's ICT Supply Chain Framework, according to the European Parliament. They questioned the identification of key ICT assets and high-risk suppliers, governance, the use of implementing acts and the high-risk supplier identification mechanism. In July, the Council sanctioned Z-Pentest leader Yuliya Vladimirovna Pankratova and hacker Denis Olegovich Degtyarenko. Both are members of CARR, a group connected to Russian military intelligence, the GRU, and responsible for cyberattacks on EU critical infrastructure and institutions.

In July the Council imposed restrictive measures on nine Russian persons and four organisations for malicious cyber activities against the EU and its member states. As of 3 November, no step in the 2026/2948(RSP) procedure had been recorded yet. The file is an RSP procedure, Parliament-only, which is why Cellar, the EU's official legal database, holds no dossier for it.
