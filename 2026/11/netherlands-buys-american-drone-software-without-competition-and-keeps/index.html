Netherlands buys American drone software without competition and keeps parliament out

The defence ministry awarded an Anduril-linked contract worth 3,050,000 euros through a negotiated procedure, citing crisis urgency, and did not inform parliament; Follow the Money revealed the deal.

6 November 2026

The Dutch investigative news site Follow the Money reported on 6 November 2026 that the Dutch Ministry of Defence secretly concluded contracts with the American drone maker Anduril. The ministry kept the deal from the House of Representatives because its value was under 50 million euros. According to the ministry, Anduril's Lattice platform now connects the chain of detection, tracking, identification and neutralization of unmanned aircraft.

Anduril says its products moved from contract signing to initial operational use with the Dutch military within about a month. The Dutch defence minister, Yesilgöz, and state secretary, Boswijk, wrote to parliament that the war in Ukraine has painfully made clear the necessity of sovereign technological innovation. They called it not a nice to have but a matter of life or death. According to Follow the Money, experts called the cooperation with Anduril unwise and unnecessary. They warned it increases dependence on American technology despite European alternatives. Follow the Money reported it gives Anduril staff potential access to sensitive military data.

The ministry did not come to Anduril by accident. Unidentified objects were observed around bases including Volkel in November and December of the previous year, and interception attempts failed. The ministry then increased investment in anti-drone measures. A ministry spokesperson said several providers were considered for the software, but Anduril's solution was available the fastest. There was an 'operational necessity', the spokesperson said.

The procurement itself carried no competition. On 13 July 2026, the ministry awarded the contract for the Command and Control systems to Surcom International B.V., a small firm based in Ede. The contract covers the C2 systems and components, including products from the Anduril Voyager family, and is worth 3,050,000 euros excluding VAT. It used a negotiated procedure without a prior call for competition, citing the urgency of a fixed delivery deadline arising from a crisis. The ministry said it first examined lawful acquisition options and found the periods for other procedures incompatible. Only one tender, from a small or medium-sized tenderer, arrived. No review request was filed.

The ministry said the American CCA programme develops software in an open architecture, so it can keep the option to develop software with Dutch and European knowledge institutions and industry to Dutch or European standards. About 950 million euros has been set aside for military innovation, against the 2.89 billion euros long communicated.
