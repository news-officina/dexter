Brussels blocks national social media age limits for 12 months pending EU-wide rules

The Commission's Kids Act sets 15 as the minimum age for opening an account across the EU, but Greece and Denmark say they will not wait. Austria will keep its own law ready to take effect when the standstill expires in July 2027.

2 November 2026

The European Commission put national bills restricting minors' social media, including Austria's minimum age of 14, on hold for 12 months while it drafts EU-wide rules. "The Commission has decided to postpone for 12 months any draft national laws overlapping with the future harmonized rules," it said. In July, Austria asked the Commission to check its draft amendment setting the age of 14 for TikTok, Instagram and YouTube.

The Commission's answer came in its Kids Act, proposed on 17 September 2026. It sets a uniform EU standard with separate limits for users under 13 and under 15. The act fixes 15 as the minimum age at which a minor anywhere in the Union can open an account on their own. The Commission said the standstill exists to prevent breaking up the single market, arguing platforms cannot comply with a patchwork of different national rules.

State Secretary Alexander Pröll said it is unacceptable that Brussels blocks member states in their national legislation without presenting a completed European solution of its own. Austria will continue preparing its own legislation at national level so the ban can take effect immediately when the standstill expires, its government said.

The standstill runs until 28 July 2027. Austria's law can enter into force at the earliest at the end of July 2027 without risking an infringement procedure. The Belgian, Slovak and Portuguese proposals, notified on 28 August, 10 September and 28 September 2026, fall inside it. The French, Italian and Greek proposals do not: their standstill periods had already expired when the Kids Act was presented.

Greece and Denmark have refused to wait. According to Politico, both will pursue their own bans while the Union drafts its law. The Kids Act must cover platforms including video games and AI companions, and impose safety-by-design rules alongside its age limits.
