US Treasury Secretary Bessent moves to military base over Iranian death threats

Iran has warned Europe that any part in the US blockade of its economy will be treated as an act of war, and US refuelling tankers have been diverted from RAF Mildenhall in England. Prosecutors have charged three men accused of acting for Iran in a plot against RAF Fairford.

12 November 2026

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has moved from his Washington-area home to a military base over death threats attributed to Iran, the New York Times reported, citing three informed sources. On Tuesday the US military said it had moved aircraft from a UK airbase in the wake of a threat from Iran, the Wall Street Journal reported.

Mohammad Mokhber, an adviser to Iran's supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, warned Europe that any complicity in the US blockade would be "an act of war that will be etched in the nation's historical memory". A US Treasury spokesperson said the secretary "will not be deterred by the Iranian regime's threats". The spokesperson said Bessent remains committed to Operation Economic Outcast and maintaining the blockade, "which together are collapsing Iran's economy".

Two US refuelling tankers and others based at RAF Mildenhall in eastern England diverted to Germany and other locations on Monday, the Wall Street Journal reported. On Wednesday, US prosecutors charged three men in the plot to target RAF Fairford airbase. They are accused of acting in support of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and Iraq's Kataib Hezbollah. A person familiar with the matter said security concerns around Bessent grew after the Wall Street Journal reported in January 2025 that he had bought a Georgetown mansion for $12.5 million. Its aerial photo, the person said, allowed the property to be geolocated.

Bessent has led Operation Economic Outcast, the US sanctions and economic pressure campaign against Iran, since August 2026. Its stated aim is to push Tehran to end attacks on shipping and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The New York Times reported that he joined at least six other US officials who left their Washington-area residences last year. They moved to a military base or other protected housing. Among them were Stephen Miller, now the White House chief of staff, and Pam Bondi, then the attorney general. Iran has previously denied plotting attacks on officials or people on US soil.
