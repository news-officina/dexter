North Korean hackers stole $6.3 billion from Binance, FBI and Secret Service say

Chainalysis and BleepingComputer say exchanges, stablecoin issuers and Arbitrum froze billions, including $3 billion by Binance, but the attacker still holds $2.4 billion beyond any issuer's reach. Pyongyang denies any role.

3 November 2026

The FBI and U.S. Secret Service formally alleged that North Korean hackers stole more than $6.3 billion from Binance. Chainalysis assessed on October 30, 2026, with what it called a high degree of confidence, that the theft was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean cybercrime group. Binance said it froze roughly $3 billion on-chain with blockchain partners.

BleepingComputer reported that Tether, Circle, Ripple and World Liberty Financial froze about $2.67 billion, and Arbitrum's Security Council froze 8,426 ETH and 74.6 million ARB, about $2.7 billion total.

Both frozen sums are disputed.

The attacker still holds more than 420,000 ETH and 18,270 BTC, about $2.4 billion beyond any issuer's reach.

Binance said the attacker used a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop to reach a service container and submit unauthorised withdrawal transactions. As an immediate precaution, it temporarily suspended withdrawals while completing security checks. "Binance will cover all losses from the SAFU fund and the company's funds. User funds are safe," the company stated. "We will also publish an updated Proof of Reserves once the remediation is complete," the company stated.

Binance said it engaged law enforcement and blockchain intelligence partners to trace and recover the funds. In an update on October 30, 2026, it stated that deposits remain unaffected and operate as normal. The theft surpasses the $1.5 billion Bybit hack attributed to the same TraderTraitor group in February 2025, according to the Associated Press, BleepingComputer and Chainalysis.

The FBI, the Secret Service and the IRS urged crypto industry entities, including THORNode and RPC node operators and DeFi services, to block transactions with the TraderTraitor actors' laundering wallets. Pyongyang denied any role. In a KCNA statement, Kim Yo-jong called the FBI's claims absurd rhetoric bringing nothing new and cited earlier American accusations over the February 2025 Bybit hack.
