Microsoft outage hit email and Teams across Europe, exposing UK police and NHS reliance

Mapping found 46 of 48 UK police forces and 62 percent of NHS trusts route email through Microsoft 365. Germany's BSI advised backing up critical services with alternatives.

13 November 2026, updated 14 November 2026

Correction: An earlier headline said police and NHS systems were hit. The sources map their reliance on Microsoft; they do not report damage.

A Microsoft 365 failure that began on 12 November knocked out email, Teams, SharePoint, Copilot and other cloud services across Europe and beyond. The disruption started in the Americas and spread worldwide. Microsoft said it had "no evidence of malicious activity associated with this incident."

Computerweekly's DNS mapping found 46 of 48 UK police forces, 95.8 percent, route email through Microsoft 365, and 47 connect to at least one US hyperscaler. For the NHS, Computerweekly's mapping found the same pattern in 132 of 213 trusts, 62 percent, for email and identity, with 165, or 77 percent, connected to a hyperscaler.

The Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) advised that business-critical services be backed up with alternatives wherever possible. Microsoft described the failure as "a large number of servers being unexpectedly unavailable"; the cause remains under investigation.

Microsoft tied the start of the failure to a network configuration change it reverted at 01.06 GMT on 13 November. The company said the strain came not from a rise in traffic but from servers that became unavailable, leaving the rest of the infrastructure to carry the load. Recovery moved unevenly: North America improved broadly, Europe recovered across much of the region, and Asia Pacific showed encouraging signals. Japan remained comparatively more stable, though some users still faced intermittent access and delayed email.

Nicky Stewart, senior advisor to the Open Cloud Coalition, said the outage is a reminder of the real economic and human costs of customer lock-in and dependency. Stewart said greater diversity across cloud providers is essential: "Over-reliance on a single provider leaves businesses and public services vulnerable to disruption and creates wider economic risks." Microsoft has promised a full post-incident report with root cause and defence-in-depth actions once mitigation is complete.
